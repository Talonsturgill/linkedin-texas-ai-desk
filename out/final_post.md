Dave Clark chose to move Auger's headquarters to Dallas on August 10th, 2026.
The bet puts customer work in North Texas while keeping engineering in Bellevue.

Clark is Auger's founder and CEO. The company builds an autonomous operating system that connects fragmented supply chain systems so AI can make and execute routine inventory, vendor and shipping decisions while people handle harder problems.

The location decision follows the same logic. Auger built its technical organization in the Seattle area, where it still has a major engineering and data science office. Clark could have kept the headquarters, customer management and go to market work beside that base.

He chose a split operating structure instead. Dallas gets the headquarters, customer facing teams and part of the science organization. Bellevue keeps the larger technical operation.

The case for Dallas is specific. Clark told two independent outlets that the region offers supply chain talent, proximity to customers and a central location for national travel. D Magazine also reported that Auger did not seek state or local incentives for the move.

The execution evidence is still early. D Magazine described an opening team of about a dozen with a path to roughly thirty by year end. Two days later, GeekWire reported about fifteen people in Dallas and a plan to add another twenty to thirty by the middle of next year.

Those accounts point in the same direction, but they are not the same staffing target. Auger's own company update says Dallas will house customer facing teams and part of its science organization. Its fetched LinkedIn profile still listed Bellevue as headquarters.

That makes the assessment mixed. The choice matches distinct work with distinct labor markets, and it preserves the engineering organization already built in Bellevue. It also creates a management test for a company whose product promises to remove coordination costs from complex operations.

The next check comes at year end. Does the Dallas office reach roughly thirty people, do public company records consistently identify it as headquarters, and do the hires actually span customer work and science? A headquarters label is easy. A working division of labor across two cities is the real decision.

Which year end measure would best show that Auger's Dallas move changed the operating company, not only its address?
