Pat Becker is backing off-grid AI power in West Texas with a fresh funding round.
The test is whether TAR can turn its factory plan into working power.

On September 10th, 2026, TAR announced a $120 million Series A led by Spark Capital. Becker is a co-founder of the Austin company. The money will help expand its West Texas manufacturing and logistics operation, grow its offices and speed work already underway.

TAR is choosing to own the job from site selection through design, buying equipment, construction and operation. Becker ties that choice to the pace needed to serve AI data centers. The alternative is to coordinate those stages across separate firms.

That is a clear execution bet. Fewer handoffs could help a project move faster. They also put more of the delivery burden on the company making the promise. A funding round gives it room to attempt that work. It does not establish that the system can serve a customer at the promised pace.

The power choice adds another test. The founders told Latitude Media they plan extra solar and battery capacity, with gas held for emergencies. Other off-grid builders lean more heavily on gas. More solar and storage require land and capital, while gas exposes operators to fuel costs and pipeline needs.

For West Texas, the near-term stake is a manufacturing base serving AI power projects. TAR says it is finishing its Terminal One manufacturing and logistics center. Latitude reports that the first plant is expected to finish this month. That gives readers a dated check before judging the broader growth story.

The assessment is mixed. Control of the full build offers a coherent way to reduce coordination delays. Yet equipment lead times still matter. The customer is unnamed, and the founders did not answer Latitude's questions about their future project pipeline.

Their aim to build in under six months remains a target. The next proof should pair factory completion with evidence that equipment works in the field. A useful customer report would show uptime, gas use and what the delivered power actually costs. Those checks would help separate a fast build from a durable service.
