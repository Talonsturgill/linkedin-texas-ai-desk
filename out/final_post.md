Chad Seely at ERCOT moved large-load verification to the front of the line.

The sequence now decides which Texas data center requests keep their place in the queue.

ERCOT answered Governor Abbott's August 3rd, 2026 directive for a comprehensive verification and audit of data centers in the interconnection process. ERCOT issued a Market Notice on August 3rd, 2026 that paused the Batch Zero study process. On August 10th, 2026 it asked the Public Utility Commission for good cause exceptions to PGRR 145, including relief from the August 7th classification deadline.

Classification decides how each large load is treated in Batch Zero. ERCOT sorts every load into one of three groups, which are base load, studied load, and a load that does not qualify. The request asks to conditionally classify large loads by August 31st, 2026, so eligibility checks finish before final classification. ERCOT's August 20th, 2026 presentation says the verification gate comes first for that reason.

Seely is ERCOT's senior vice president of regulatory policy and general counsel. He told the Texas Tribune that verification would come before the interconnection study begins. He told Utility Dive that ERCOT will not finish the Batch Zero study by April 9th, 2027, and that its goal is a December 10th, 2026 filing with the Public Utility Commission.

The alternative was real. ERCOT could have kept the August 7th deadline in the Planning Guide and classified large loads before the eligibility checks were complete. It chose delay instead, and that choice turns a classification date into a verification gate.

The scale is why this is important to the state. The audit covers about 250 to 300 projects, most of them data centers, representing about 200 gigawatts of future demand. Projects that fail the audit are to be denied grid connection. Large loads that do not pass verification, or do not respond, are disqualified from Batch Zero.

This is a public grid process, so the test is accountability. Did the schedule hold, and did the criteria get disclosed? ERCOT expects to send requests for information in late August and early September, with possible further rounds in October and November. Seely said ERCOT is still working on a new Batch Zero timeline.

The next check is the Batch Zero Eligibility Verification Report that ERCOT plans to file by December 10th, 2026. Will the December filing name the disqualification criteria, so a developer can check its own load against them before the study deadline?
